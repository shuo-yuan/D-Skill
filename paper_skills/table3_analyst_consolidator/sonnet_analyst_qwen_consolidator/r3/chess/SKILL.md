## When NOT to Analyze: Fast Paths and Stopping Rules

Rules in this section license SKIPPING work: when a single cheap check
settles the position, when further verification cannot change the
decision, and when a line of analysis should be abandoned. Every entry
must name (a) the exact trigger that makes the shortcut safe and
(b) the condition under which the shortcut must NOT be taken. A rule
without both parts does not belong here.


- **Mate Shortcut (`#`)**: When legal-move list annotates a move with `#`, treat as absolute top-priority. **Verify first**: Run `<safety>` or `<simulate>` to confirm geometry and defender counts before committing. Do not skip tool verification based on manual reads. **Lock to verified mate**: Do not drift to other candidates after verification; commit the exact move the tool confirmed immediately.
- **Forced/Obvious Moves**: If only one legal move exists, or a move wins decisive material/avoids immediate loss, commit immediately without deeper simulation.
- **Forced Recapture After Check**: When opponent has only 1–3 legal moves to recapture/block your check, skip heavy tool analysis. Compare remaining moves by immediate material/safety consequence and commit the best resolution.
- **No Re-litigation Loops**: Once key facts are verified a single time via tool or careful reading, do not re-derive or second-guess them. If a second check feels necessary, invoke `<safety>` or `<simulate>` once. Ask: "Did I receive new board information since my last check?" If no, commit immediately.
