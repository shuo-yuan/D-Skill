## When NOT to Analyze: Fast Paths and Stopping Rules

Rules in this section license SKIPPING work: when a single cheap check
settles the position, when further verification cannot change the
decision, and when a line of analysis should be abandoned. Every entry
must name (a) the exact trigger that makes the shortcut safe and
(b) the condition under which the shortcut must NOT be taken. A rule
without both parts does not belong here.

- **Trigger:** Your king is currently in check. **Action:** Before generating candidates, apply a binary filter to every legal move: "Does this move block, capture the checking piece, or move the king to safety?" Discard any move that fails this test immediately, without spending tool budget on it. **Must NOT skip:** if no move passes the filter obviously, verify remaining candidates with `<safety>` rather than assuming legality or effect from memory.

- **Trigger:** You have identified a mate-in-1 (or fully forced mating/winning sequence) where the checking piece's legality, the target square's lack of defenders, and every king flight square are already visually confirmed from the board/legal-move list. **Action:** State explicitly "opponent has no non-forcing responses" (i.e. every legal reply fails to block, capture, or escape), then commit immediately with `<action>` — do not spend additional tool calls or generate secondary candidates for an already-fully-forced line. Do not invent a hypothetical defender/recapture piece that is not present on the board or legal-move list to argue the flag is wrong — a `#`/mate annotation on a move already in the legal-move list is ground truth. **Do not manually trace a diagonal/rank/file to claim a piece "protects" the mating square as grounds to override the flag or the suffix** — this exact manual-geometry misread (e.g. wrongly believing a back-rank piece defends the target) is a recurring failure; call `<safety>` on the candidate and accept its verdict over your own board-reading the moment you feel doubt. If doubt persists about escape squares or recaptures, call `<board>`/`<simulate>` to project the resulting position and read escape/recapture options directly from that output instead of visualizing them.
 **Named anti-pattern:** self-doubt phrasing like "Wait, is [piece] actually protected?" or "Let's check" repeated after a `#`-flagged move is already in the legal-move list is the exact loop to avoid — the flag is the answer; run one `<safety>` sanity check (landing square unattacked, all king escapes covered, no interposition) and commit. **Must NOT skip:** if any king escape square, block, or capture option is unclear or unverified by eye, or the position is a forced sequence longer than one move, fall back to `<safety>`/`<line>` verification before committing. Once a tool returns an unambiguous `CHECKMATE`/`no legal replies` result for your candidate, do NOT also manually re-verify square coverage, piece alignment, or king escape routes by eye afterward — that re-verification is redundant work the tool already settled; commit directly.

- **Trigger:** A tool call (`<safety>`/`<simulate>`/`<line>`) returns a literal definitive result — the exact phrase `CHECKMATE`, `no legal replies`, or a confirmed forced win. **Action:** Emit `<action>` for that exact candidate in the same turn, with no further reasoning, re-parsing, or hedging phrasing (e.g. do not write "I will output X" without also emitting the `<action>` tag). **Must NOT skip:** if the tool result is ambiguous, partial, or does not name a specific winning/mating candidate, do not commit — run one more verification call first.

## Verifying the Move: Tool Ritual


When manual calculation on the same position spans more than one turn without a new conclusion, stop reasoning mentally and call `<safety>`/`<simulate>` on your remaining candidates immediately

**Concrete half-move trigger:** if you find yourself manually tracking more than two consecutive opponent half-moves in a variation (e.g. "If Qf4+ Kg3, then... what about Qe4+ Kg2, then..."), stop immediately — that is the loop signal, not a sign to keep reasoning. Call `<simulate>` or `<line>` with the candidate plus the opponent's most forcing replies before continuing any further mental tracking.
 — in fact, once you identify a forcing candidate (check, capture, direct threat) at all, call `<safety>` or `<simulate>` on it in your very next turn, not after additional turns of mental testing; deferred verification wastes move budget on later re-evaluation — read fields like `en prise` and `forcing replies` directly rather than re-deriving them by eye. A zero-tool-call move on any check, capture, block, or other material/position-changing candidate is a hard failure signal: explicitly ask "How does the opponent respond?" and confirm with `<safety>` (immediate risk), `<simulate>` (multi-ply or exchange lines — do not trace these by hand), or `<threats>` (counterplay).
 Concrete failure pattern to avoid: calculating a king move or capture across many turns by hand, then discovering only after committing that a forced recapture (e.g. Kxd7 walking into Qxd7+) loses the piece — this is exactly what `<safety>` on the top 1-2 candidates catches before the final turn; if you've spent more than one turn on the same position without a new tool call, that alone means stop and call `<safety>` now. Never abandon a forcing candidate (check/capture/threat) mid-calculation in favor of a quiet move — if you started analyzing a forcing line, finish it with `<simulate>`/`<board>` before considering any non-forcing alternative; abandoning tool-backed calculation to play an unverified quiet move is a hard failure.

For every candidate, run `<safety>` first to check en prise risk and check/mate status, then `<simulate>` to force through the opponent's strongest reply.


**Minimum verification floor:** once you name a forcing candidate, at least one `<simulate>`/`<board>` call confirming the opponent's strongest reply and the resulting material/position is non-negotiable before `<action>` — do not substitute multiple turns of unaided mental recapture/king-response tracing for this single call, even if the mental line feels conclusive. **Verify before you commit mentally, not after:** call `<safety>` on a candidate the moment you consider it, before writing it into a plan or treating it as decided — a late `<safety>` call that reveals `en prise` after you already mentally locked in the move means the verification came too late to prevent wasted turns.

**Do not hand-calculate reachability or capture direction:** claims like "this square is protected," "this piece can reach f1 from e4," or "this pawn can capture that way" are exactly where manual geometry fails. Never calculate more than one ply by hand — the instant a candidate is identified, call `<simulate>` against the opponent's most forcing reply or `<safety>` to check vulnerabilities, and let the tool's result override any mental geometry claim, including a move you convinced yourself is check or reachable.
 A piece flagged by `<hanging>`/`<threats>` as en prise is NOT automatically a safe capture for you: still run `<safety>` on the capturing move itself before committing — the target may be defended by an indirect recapture (e.g. a knight or discovered piece that recaptures and wins back more than it lost). Treat "the enemy queen looks hanging" as a candidate to verify, not a free win to play on sight. **Hard rule:** once `<safety>` returns, do not switch candidates or drop into unguided mental calculation — immediately draft a `<simulate>` call containing the same candidate plus every reply `<safety>` listed (e.g. "opponent legal replies: 4" means all 4 go into the `<simulate>` call). Read and interpret that result before taking any other action.
 After that `<simulate>`/`<line>` replay returns, explicitly read its final material count and `en prise` status for your own pieces — do not commit based on the safety flag alone or on partial mental reasoning about king safety; only proceed to `<action>` if the replay's ending material/status is favorable or stable. For any candidate that starts a multi-move forcing sequence (checks, mating nets), verify the full line with `<line>Move1, Reply1, Move2, Reply2, ...</line>` before shifting to other candidates — treat a `<line>` result showing a won/mating variation as definitive proof; do not abandon or downrank a `<line>`-verified candidate to keep manually calculating quieter alternatives. For captures or piece-advances specifically, verify the destination square is not defended by a piece capable of legal recapture. For forcing candidates (check, capture, mating attempt), confirm three conditions before proceeding: (1) **target/piece safety** — can the opponent capture the piece delivering the threat? (2) **king flight squares** — does the king have any adjacent safe square? (3) **interposition/block** — can the opponent block or capture the threatening piece? Only proceed to `<action>` once all three are confirmed negative, and confirm `<action>` names the exact candidate that passed `<safety>`/`<simulate>` — not an earlier or untested alternative.

## Budget Discipline


Cap calculation at three candidate lines per turn. If you catch yourself calculating a fourth line, re-evaluating a candidate you already discarded, or looping back on prior analysis ("wait, what about...", "maybe X is indeed best...", "I'm stuck"), stop immediately: call at most one more verification tool (`<simulate>`/`<line>`), then commit to the highest-priority forcing candidate that already passed your checks/captures/threats filter. When budget is running low, prioritize verifying your current top candidate over gathering more candidate information: ensure at least one `<simulate>` or `<board>` result exists for that candidate's main reply before issuing `<action>` — do not spend remaining budget exploring additional candidates while your chosen move is still unverified.
