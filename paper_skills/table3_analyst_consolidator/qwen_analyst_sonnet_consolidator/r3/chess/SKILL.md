## When NOT to Analyze: Fast Paths and Stopping Rules

Rules in this section license SKIPPING work: when a single cheap check
settles the position, when further verification cannot change the
decision, and when a line of analysis should be abandoned. Every entry
must name (a) the exact trigger that makes the shortcut safe and
(b) the condition under which the shortcut must NOT be taken. A rule
without both parts does not belong here.


**Fast path — forced mate-in-1:** When the legal-move list explicitly flags a candidate `#`, skip material recalculation and full FEN/board reconstruction entirely — do not recount pieces or re-verify the position from scratch. Instead run only this targeted 3-point audit: (1) confirm the checking piece is supported/cannot be captured favorably, (2) list the opponent king's flight squares and confirm each is covered/blocked/off-board, (3) confirm no piece can interpose. If all three hold, commit immediately via `<action>`. Do NOT take this shortcut if the move list shows more than one `#`-flagged move, or if `<safety>`/`<simulate>` output contradicts the flag — in that case fall back to full verification.


**General rule for any check/capture verification (not just mate-in-1):** once a candidate is named, check only the critical constraints — king flight squares, whether the checking/attacking piece can be captured, whether any piece can interpose or defend — rather than manually re-tracing every ray, diagonal, or square-by-square geometry. Exhaustive geometric enumeration is slower and more error-prone than the targeted constraint test and should be avoided even outside the mate-in-1 case.

**Recurring scan trigger:** after every tool response that returns a new position (post-`<simulate>`, post-opponent-reply, or any refreshed legal-move list), re-scan that list for a `#` symbol before doing anything else — don't only check for mate at the start of a turn. If found, verify the mating net and prioritize committing it over continuing a speculative line.


**Fast path — any fully self-contained forcing line (not just mate-in-1):** if a candidate's tactical outcome is completely determined by immediate, unambiguous piece interactions with no opponent recourse or branching (e.g. an immediate decisive capture with zero legal defenses), commit after the same targeted audit style as mate-in-1 (verify the specific squares/pieces involved) rather than opening `<safety>`/`<simulate>` purely as a formality. Do NOT take this shortcut if any ambiguity exists about legality, defender count, or opponent recourse — fall back to full tool verification. (This is the sole exception to the Hard Gate in "Verifying the Move".)
