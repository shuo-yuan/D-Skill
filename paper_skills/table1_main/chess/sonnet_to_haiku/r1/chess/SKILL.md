## Critical Rules (MUST follow)


- Before any other analysis, scan the legal-move list for moves flagged `#` (checkmate) or capturing undefended/high-value material. Treat `#`/`+` as a hint to verify, not proof: state in one sentence which piece guards the mating square and which escape squares the enemy king lacks (the motif — back-rank, boxed king, overloaded defender, etc.), then call `<board>`/`<simulate>`/`<line>` on that exact move and confirm the output explicitly states CHECKMATE (or zero legal replies for the opponent) before playing it. NEVER skip this tool call because the move notation already looks terminal (e.g., contains "#" or appears to win a queen) — a move that looks obviously decisive still requires this same one tool call before PLAY. Do not invent or guess opponent replies to "double-check" mate — guessed replies are often illegal and waste a tool call; if you want extra verification, use a single-move board/line tool on your own move only. If a tool reports a terminal/decisive result alongside a seemingly contradictory flag (e.g. "pieces en prise", "exchange risk"), do not silently ignore it — state explicitly in your WHY why it doesn't apply, or re-query before committing. Once the motif is named and confirmation obtained, skip further threat/safety scans that are moot given the forced mate. Runtime gate: before writing PLAY on any believed mate/decisive-win move, scan the tool's returned text for an explicit no-legal-reply/CHECKMATE confirmation string produced this turn — visual obviousness alone is NOT sufficient grounds to commit.

- When the legal-move list already shows a `#` suffix, treat that as a KNOWN fact, not an open question — spend exactly ONE targeted confirmation call, calling `<board>`/`<line>` on ONLY that exact move with NO opponent replies appended (do not pass hypothetical replies to `<simulate>`; there are none after mate and this will error out and waste the call). This single call is cheap, mandatory overhead, not wasted budget — never skip it to save the call, and never spend MORE than one call confirming an already-`#`-flagged move. The instant you spot a `#`-marked move in the list, STOP screening other candidates (do not also batch-test unrelated checks like `Qxf8+`/`Qg7+` in the same `<safety>` call) — the marked move is already the answer; spend your one call confirming only it, then PLAY. EXCEPTION (proven economical): if an EARLIER `<line>`/`<simulate>` call THIS TURN already played out the sequence reaching this exact position and its output showed the opponent in check with no legal escape (or the move itself appeared with `#` inside that output), that earlier call already satisfies the confirmation requirement — do not spend a second call re-verifying the same move; play it directly from the legal-move list.
- Before calling any tool with a candidate move, check it character-for-character against the printed "Legal moves" list. Only query moves that appear verbatim there — illegal-move queries waste scarce tool calls.
- NEVER commit a move if the latest `<safety>`/`<simulate>` output flags it "en prise" or "LOSING EXCHANGE RISK" for your own piece, unless a deeper `<simulate>`/`<line>` confirms the material total after the opponent's capture is acceptable and no alternative candidate avoids/better-compensates the loss. This veto is PERMANENT for the rest of the turn: once ANY candidate is confirmed material-losing by your own tool output, do not circle back and commit it later just because a different candidate's follow-up verification seemed inconclusive or got abandoned mid-check — if you abandoned verifying a clean candidate (zero attackers/defenders, no en prise), finish verifying THAT one to a concrete end state before ever reconsidering the already-flagged loser. If budget runs out before this verification is possible, do NOT commit the flagged move — fall back to re-playing an earlier move you already verified as safe/winning this turn instead. A bare risk flag is NOT a verdict and "it's check/forcing" is NEVER sufficient justification to override it — a flagged check can still simply lose the piece with no compensation (e.g. Rf5+ flagged "attackers 1 vs defenders 0" followed by forced Qxf5 just loses the rook). You MUST run `<simulate>`/`<line>` on that exact candidate plus the opponent's flagged forcing reply and read the resulting material count before deciding. Once a `<simulate>` confirms a large material loss (or that the move fails to address an existing mate threat), discard that candidate PERMANENTLY — do not re-test it with more `<safety>`/`<board>`/`<simulate>` calls, and do not play it anyway. Move your remaining budget to untested candidates. This check applies to the FINAL move you commit, not just early candidates. Before writing PLAY on any capture/check, especially the move that "finishes" the puzzle, run `<safety>` on that exact move (batched with any other legal capture/check, e.g. `<safety>Qxa2,Qxf1+</safety>`) and confirm it is not immediately recapturable for no net gain before committing.

- When writing the final `<action>`, copy the move string exactly as it appears in the legal-move list or the tool output that confirmed it (including any `+`/`#`/`=` suffix) — never retype a shortened version. Diff your action string character-by-character against that source before submitting. Final check: ask "does this exact string, including any trailing symbol, appear verbatim in the legal-moves list?" — if the legal-move list shows `Qf2#` and you type `Qf2`, that is a mismatch; fix it before submitting.

- MANDATORY FIRST ACTION every turn, before any candidate search: call `<threats>`/`<hanging>` with no arguments
 — if this call lists ANY opponent piece as en prise, that capture is your DEFAULT top candidate: run `<simulate>` on it FIRST and confirm it is safe/winning before spending any call on a risky check (especially one your own later `<safety>` flags "LOSING EXCHANGE RISK"). Do not pivot away from a confirmed free-material capture to chase unverified checks — repeatedly doing so and never playing the hanging-piece capture is a confirmed failure mode. Also, when this call (or any `<simulate>`/`<board>`/`<safety>` call) flags one of YOUR OWN pieces as "en prise," identify the SPECIFIC attacker/threat causing it (which enemy piece/pawn, which capture) before picking your move — a threat against your own material (e.g. a 7th-rank pawn forking two pieces) can outweigh an attacking idea you already favor; compare the two explicitly in your WHY. to see which pieces on BOTH sides are currently en prise or forcing, AND what the opponent's best reply would be if you passed (mate threats included) — before running `<safety>` on check/capture candidates. Use this tool output, not visual impression, to pick the concrete target for your first candidate and to reframe the turn correctly (e.g. "defend my hanging piece while finding the best forcing continuation" rather than jumping straight to an attack) — this proven opening habit (confirmed repeatedly across successful traces) reliably surfaces undefended-piece captures and urgent mate threats (e.g. "Qxg7#") that must filter your candidate list first; never skip it even when an attacking idea seems obvious. Treat any of YOUR OWN hanging pieces or named mate threats revealed here as a hard constraint for the rest of the turn: every later candidate must defend it, trade it off profitably, or outrace it with a faster win — confirm via each candidate's `<safety>`/`<simulate>` output (not assumption) that the threat is resolved. A candidate whose output still lists that same piece en prise, or that doesn't address the named threat, has NOT solved it — discard it. Also use this same opening baseline to screen sacrificial candidates: if a sacrifice would expose a piece that the turn-opening `<hanging>`/`<threats>` call showed as currently safe, require a `<simulate>`/`<line>` showing concrete compensation before considering it. Once a standing opponent threat/check named by this opening call is confirmed harmless via one `<line>`/`<simulate>` (e.g. a clean recapture refutes it), carry that verified harmless status forward for the rest of the turn — treat it as a known, resolved resource rather than re-testing it from scratch against every later candidate; just confirm each new candidate doesn't newly expose the same square to it.
- When calling `<simulate>`/`<line>`/`<board>` with opponent replies, prefer replies already confirmed legal by a prior tool result. If no legal-reply list exists yet for the position, it is acceptable (and a proven successful habit) to probe with several plausible candidate replies in one `<simulate>` call, including ones you're unsure are legal — read any "not a legal move" errors to prune down to the real forced-reply set. But once a call returns an error or an explicit legal-replies list, every SUBSEQUENT call for that position must reuse that confirmed set verbatim — do not keep re-guessing after the real list is known.

Runtime gate (proven habit — always apply, even when the mate looks visually obvious): before writing PLAY on any believed mate/decisive-win move, call a tool (`<safety>`/`<board>`/`<line>`) on that exact move and scan its returned text for an explicit no-legal-reply/CHECKMATE confirmation string produced THIS turn. Committing an unverified mate without that tool call is never correct, even if it would have worked out — always verify first, regardless of confidence.


Once a single tool call's output for your exact candidate already contains an explicit terminal confirmation (e.g. "CHECKMATE — opponent has no legal replies"), that candidate is VERIFIED — do not spend a second tool call re-asking the same question (e.g. "confirm Qh1 is mate") before PLAY. One confirming call is both necessary and sufficient; re-verifying an already-confirmed terminal result wastes budget for zero new information.

- If a `<line>`/`<simulate>` call on your intended mating move returns a status OTHER than CHECKMATE (e.g. "opponent is in check", or lists your own piece as en prise with no forced mate follow-up), that result DISCONFIRMS the candidate — do NOT commit it on a "mental picture" of a trapped king. Either extend the line one more ply to test if mate is truly forced, or abandon the candidate and test a different one. Re-running the SAME losing sequence a second time and then committing anyway is a repeated, confirmed failure mode — never do this.

- Runtime check for the opening `<hanging>`/`<threats>` call: scan its "your side en prise" / "opponent's forcing moves" output for anything of equal or higher value than what your candidate move wins or threatens. Only proceed with that candidate if it addresses (defends/trades off) that exposure or its gain clearly outweighs it — do not pick an attacking candidate that ignores a bigger problem already visible in this first call.
## Finding the Move: Motifs and Candidates


- Step 1: check the legal-move list for `#`/`+` suffixes and undefended captures before considering motifs or threats.
 BEFORE generating a candidate list, first scan the enemy king's current escape squares and which of your pieces already restrict them — if the king is confined to very few squares (especially a corner), prioritize testing the cheapest check (minor piece/pawn) that leaves exactly ONE legal reply square over queen checks/sacrifices; a single-reply check is the hallmark of a forced mating sequence and should be tested first with `<line>`, not discovered last after cycling through queen-sac candidates. If mate (`#`) is available, skip straight to verifying that exact move with one tool call, confirming CHECKMATE (or zero legal replies) before committing. A check is NOT inherently better than an available winning capture — scan EVERY capture in the legal-move list (not just checks/sacrifices) and cross-check each target square against `<threats>`/`<safety>` for whether it's defended; an undefended-piece capture often ranks ABOVE an unclear forcing check unless `<simulate>`/`<line>` concretely shows the check nets strictly more material or forces mate. If any move is BOTH a capture AND a check, test it first with `<safety>` or `<line>` before any quieter alternative — capture+check moves are the most informative first probe since they narrow the opponent's replies most.
- Generate candidates by enumerating the actual legal-move list and the opponent's full reply list from tool output — never guess from the printed board alone. Before narrowing to one candidate, extract EVERY check and capture from the legal-move list and run them together in a single batched `<safety>` call (e.g. `<safety>Rh8+,Bf7+,Nf7,Bh7+</safety>`); never drill deep on the first check you notice while leaving other legal checks untested — a later-listed check is often the actual mating move.
- Discard any candidate check/capture if the survey shows it is answered by a single non-losing recapture that nets your attacker for nothing extra — don't spend simulate calls confirming sacrifices that already fail this filter.
- Proven opening sequence (reconfirmed by successful traces): `<hanging>`/`<threats>` first to spot loose pieces and named threats on both sides, THEN batch every plausible check/capture (e.g. `<safety>Qh2+,Qxf2+,Bxe7</safety>`) into one `<safety>` call to compare attacker/defender counts and risk flags side-by-side before spending budget on deeper `<line>`/`<simulate>` on the best candidate(s). If `<hanging>`/`<threats>` is empty, search offensive candidates freely; if it names a threat, that threat is a standing filter — discard any later candidate whose `<safety>`/`<simulate>` result still shows it present, and re-check your final move against it before PLAY.
- Also use `<hanging>` to list which of the OPPONENT's pieces are currently undefended, and treat that list as a primary source of capturing candidates
- Read the `<threats>` "mate threats" field literally: if it says "none," do NOT default to king-hunt/sacrifice candidates. Instead scan for which of YOUR OWN pieces are hanging to a concrete enemy capture (even a quiet one like `Bxe4` winning a rook) and generate candidates that move, defend, or trade off that piece FIRST — only pursue a mating attack once that hanging piece is resolved or a `<line>`/`<simulate>` proves the sacrifice outweighs the loss. — prefer a concrete undefended target over a vaguely-described "king trapped"/"mating follow-up" narrative that isn't backed by a specific piece/square.
- Once `<hanging>`/`<threats>` reveals a capturable undefended piece, keep that capture in your candidate set for the REST of the turn, even across multiple moves of analysis. Before committing to any other move, state in one line why it beats (or loses to) the hanging-piece capture, backed by `<safety>`/`<simulate>` on both — never silently let a later idea override an earlier verified free-material finding without this comparison. Even when a piece (especially a high-value one like a queen) looks obviously hanging, run one `<safety>`/`<board>` call on that exact capture and confirm "attackers 0 vs defenders 0"/"your side en prise: none" before playing it — a visually undefended piece can still have a hidden defender or forced recapture that loses more than it gains.

- Before committing any mating or king-trapping candidate, enumerate EVERY square adjacent to the enemy king and state for each one whether it is occupied (by own/enemy piece), attacked by one of your pieces, or blocked by the enemy's own piece/pawn — do this from board/piece placement, never from a `+`/`#` suffix in the move list alone. Name the specific piece/pawn responsible for each blocked/attacked square (e.g. "king trapped on f8: g8 blocked by own rook, g7 occupied by own pawn, e8/e7 covered by Qh8"), AND name the piece that supports/backs your checking piece if one exists (e.g. "Qh6 mates because rook g8 blocks the king's flight and Rh1 backs the h-file") — a bare conclusion like "king trapped in corner" without naming these concrete features is insufficient even if the move later verifies as correct. A `#` in the legal-move list is a prompt to do this reconstruction, not a substitute for it. If you cannot name the reason for every adjacent square, re-scan the board before trusting the candidate or calling a tool on it.

- Before simulating any candidate, scan explicitly for a move that is forcing AND has a second purpose (check + fork/skewer/discovered attack on an undefended or overloaded piece); name this double-purpose motif in your WHY line if one exists. Test THIS candidate first with one `<simulate>`/`<line>` call rather than iterating sequentially through other checks/captures — committing early to the motif-matching candidate conserves budget to fully verify the finishing move, instead of exhausting calls on unrelated dead-end checks before reaching it.

- Before drilling deep on any single piece's checks (e.g. repeated queen-sac ideas), do ONE pass over the full legal-move list and group ALL checks/captures by piece and by the king-safety feature each exploits (removes the same escape square, forks, etc.). If a cheap, non-sacrificial check (rook/minor/pawn) targets the same escape squares as a sacrificial queen candidate, test the cheap one FIRST with `<line>` — it costs the same one tool call and is more often both forcing and loss-free. Do not spend 5+ calls cycling variations of one piece's idea while an untested check from another piece sits unused in the legal-move list.
- When `<threats>` names a SPECIFIC mate threat (e.g. "Qxg7#"), do not default to the first checking move you find just because it gives check. Build a short list of candidates that plausibly neutralize THAT exact threat (capture/block the threatening piece, guard the mating square, remove the attacker, or create a bigger counter-threat), verify each with `<safety>`/`<line>`, and commit only to whichever verified candidate leaves the best material/mate result. A check that does not address the named threat is not a valid candidate regardless of how forcing it looks.

- Even when a tactical motif (e.g. an undefended piece) jumps out immediately, do NOT lock onto it as the final answer — first enumerate the OTHER checks/captures in the legal-move list and briefly compare: does any alternative win more material or mate faster? Only after confirming your spotted candidate is at least as good as the alternatives should you verify it with a tool and commit. Relying solely on a tool's post-hoc confirmation without this comparison step is not sufficient, even if the move happens to be correct.


- Reuse the turn-opening `<threats>` call (see Critical Rules) to see what the opponent would do if you passed — not just your own attacking ideas. Filter every candidate against whether it parries each listed threat (moves the piece, defends it, or creates a bigger counter-threat); discard in your WHY any candidate that ignores a named threat, even if it looks tactically appealing otherwise.

- When a `<threats>`/`<safety>`/`<simulate>` result names a specific tactical motif (pin, fork, overload, undefended piece sharing a line/diagonal/file with a high-value target), immediately commit your NEXT 2-3 tool calls to driving that exact candidate to a concrete verified endpoint (CHECKMATE or final material count) before testing unrelated candidates.
- When scanning for checks/captures on a key defender (e.g. a pawn shielding the enemy king), do not stop at the first piece type you notice (e.g. only queen moves) — explicitly check the legal-move list for OTHER pieces (rook, knight, bishop, pawn) that also check or capture that same defender/square; include at least 2-3 distinct piece types as candidates before narrowing, since a cheaper sacrifice from a different piece can be the one that actually forces mate.

- Proven pattern: when the enemy king is boxed by its own undeveloped pawns with few escape squares, enumerate EVERY available check from the legal-move list and batch them in one `<safety>`/`<simulate>` call; scan results first for any entry stating CHECKMATE/no-legal-replies before comparing exchange-risk flags on the rest — this converges fastest on mating nets in cramped-king positions.
## Verifying the Move: Tool Ritual


When a `<safety>` risk tag or a `<simulate>` result shows a material deficit after only the FIRST capture/recapture, do not decide yet — this is a mid-sequence snapshot, not a verdict. Issue one more `<simulate>`/`<board>` call that extends the sequence through YOUR follow-up recapture/response, and only keep or discard the candidate once you see the material count at the true end of the forced chain (no further forced recaptures pending on either side).


Before committing any move that starts a forced sequence (check, capture, or threat inviting forced replies), use `<line>` or chained `<simulate>` calls to play it through EVERY forced reply until no forced recapture remains, then read the material count at that FINAL node — never judge from an intermediate one-ply snapshot (e.g. simulate the full `Bxf3+, Qxf3, gxf3`, not just `Bxf3, Qxf3`). When your candidate sets up a follow-up (fork, skewer, discovery, pin), simulate your move, the forced reply, AND the follow-up capture before committing — never commit on the initial threat alone.

Run `<safety>`/`<simulate>` on every check and capture candidate before committing, first reading the "opponent legal replies" count/list to confirm which reply is forced or best (do not assume), then checking the "attackers vs defenders" count and "pieces en prise" list; this proven opener (`<hanging>`/`<threats>` then `<safety>`) must be followed by simulating EVERY legal reply listed, not just the one you expect — a reply you didn't anticipate can still refute the candidate; if the move is forcing and the material swing is large, don't stop at the surface flag — escalate to `<simulate>`/`<line>` with concrete named move sequences (e.g. `<simulate>Rg5, Bxg3+,Qg6,Nd3,Qh6+,Rd4+</simulate>`) rather than evaluating by mental reasoning, and confirm any claimed threat actually appears in the tool's reported reply list. Apply this to every candidate, not just the first.

Before writing PLAY, scan your WHY line for verification words ('confirmed', 'mate', 'wins material', 'forced'). If present, confirm an actual `<board>`, `<line>`, or `<simulate>` call this turn produced output supporting that exact claim — if not, call the tool first; never assert a confirmation you haven't obtained. If the tool reports "check" where you expected "mate," or flags your own piece as en prise after your intended finisher, this DISCONFIRMS the plan — do not relabel it as mate or commit it; find another continuation or fall back to a simpler, already-confirmed material-winning candidate.

When a forcing check succeeds, immediately test the single most natural follow-up (the move that restricts the enemy king furthest along the same file/rank/diagonal, e.g. a rook/queen lift to the cut-off square) with one `<line>` call BEFORE trying alternative or speculative second moves — if it confirms mate or a clearly winning result, stop and play it; only branch into other candidates if that first verification fails.

When the position updates after an opponent's forced reply, before committing the next move check whether this exact continuation was already verified in an earlier `<simulate>`/`<line>` call that reached a concrete favorable material count or mate; if so, play that same move — do not invent a fresh, unverified candidate at the critical finishing step. When supplying opponent replies to `<simulate>`/`<board>`/`<line>`, use ONLY moves confirmed legal by a prior tool result (legal-move list, a `<safety>`/`<threats>` reply list, or a previous error message listing real replies) — never guess a plausible-sounding reply. If a call errors (e.g. "X is not a legal move here; opponent's legal replies: A, B, C"), your VERY NEXT call must reuse exactly that listed set verbatim — do not invent a different square or retry with another guess; treat the error's reply list as ground truth, not a prompt to re-guess. Never pass more opponent replies than the tool's documented limit (max 5 for `<simulate>`); if you have more candidates, split into separate batched calls.

Before building a multi-reply `<simulate>`/`<line>` batch, check whether a legal-replies list for that exact position was already printed by an earlier tool call this turn (e.g. from `<safety>`/`<threats>`/a prior error message) — if so, draw your reply set from THAT list verbatim rather than guessing additional squares; mixing one guessed illegal reply into an otherwise-valid batch still burns that slot for nothing.

If a `<simulate>` call already shows a UNIFORM pattern across all tested opponent replies (e.g. your piece is never en prise and the same material gain is available in every branch), confirm the concrete end-state with a SINGLE `<line>` call on one representative branch, then commit — do not re-run `<line>` on each remaining branch to re-confirm the same uniform pattern; that wastes budget needed for positions that actually differ across replies.

When simulating a forcing move, test it against EVERY legal defensive reply the opponent has (not just the first or most obvious one) by batching them in one call, e.g. `<simulate>Rf1+, Ke4,Kg4,Kg2,Ke2</simulate>`, and read the "en prise"/check/material fields for each branch separately; if any branch leaves your own piece en prise, check what your own next capture nets in that branch before deciding the line still favors you. Only mark the candidate "safe" once EVERY legal reply has been checked this way — a candidate confirmed safe against the expected reply but untested against other legal replies is not yet verified.

For any check candidate, call `<safety>` first to read the opponent's legal-reply count; if it is very low (e.g., 1), follow up with `<simulate>`/`<board>` on that exact forced reply to inspect the resulting position before committing — this confirms the check is truly forcing and shows the real follow-up position, not an assumed one.


Before any `<board>`/`<line>`/`<simulate>` call, re-read the most recent observation's legal-move list/FEN to confirm which position you are currently in, and build your move sequence starting ONLY from that current position — never re-include a move already played/confirmed, and never jump ahead to a hypothetical future position. If a call errors with "not a legal move here," treat this as a position-tracking mistake, not a syntax issue: re-check the last observation before retrying.

Never commit a forcing move on the strength of its legal-move-list label alone (e.g., trusting "Qf7#" just because it's listed with `#`). Commit only after at least one tool call for that exact sequence SUCCEEDS (no error) and its returned output explicitly shows the resulting position/material/checkmate status. This applies even to a move that looks like plain, obviously-safe free material (e.g. "that rook looks undefended now") — ZERO tool calls before PLAY on any capture or final move is never acceptable; a visual read can miss a defending pawn or distant piece that only `<hanging>`/`<safety>` would reveal.

For any checking candidate, follow this proven order: (1) `<safety>` first to confirm the check isn't losing material and to enumerate ALL legal king/defensive replies, (2) `<simulate>` on every one of those replies (batched) to see resulting material and any newly-hanging pieces, (3) only then commit the check — do this even when a reply looks obviously forced.

For any checking or sacrificial candidate, run `<board>`/`<simulate>` on that exact move first and confirm the output explicitly states "gives check: yes" (or shows the opponent's reduced legal-reply list) before treating it as forcing — do not assume a move gives check just because it looks like it does on the board. A quick `<board>` call on the opening forcing move of any line, reading "gives check: yes/no" plus immediate material/en-prise state, is a cheap way to ground the candidate before spending `<simulate>`/`<line>` calls on the deeper forced sequence.

If a call errors (e.g. "X is not a legal move here; opponent's legal replies: A, B, C"), your VERY NEXT call must reuse exactly that listed set verbatim — do not invent a different square or retry with another guess; treat the error's reply list as ground truth, not a prompt to re-guess. After re-running with the corrected reply, check whether the new output changes your plan (different material count, a newly undefended piece, no mate signal) — if so, update your candidate/WHY accordingly instead of silently falling back to your original assumption.


Mandatory literal-text check: re-read the EXACT text of the last relevant tool call's status/result before writing the WHY — if your planned claim is "forces mate" or "wins material" but the output literally says "check" (not CHECKMATE) or shows a losing/negative material swing, you MUST revise the WHY and candidate instead of stating the stronger unsupported claim.

This simulate-before-commit rule applies to QUIET moves too, not just checks/captures: if a `<board>`/`<safety>` snapshot of your intended move still lists one of your own pieces under "your side en prise," treat that as a mandatory trigger to run `<simulate>`/`<line>` with that move plus the opponent's most natural capture of the flagged piece, and read the resulting material count before committing — never commit a quiet move (e.g. a pawn push) on the theory it creates a positional asset ("passed pawn," "traps king") while an en-prise flag from the SAME snapshot goes unaddressed.

When `<safety>` on a capture/candidate reveals a forcing opponent reply (recapture, check, etc.), immediately follow up with `<simulate>` on that exact candidate plus the forcing reply and read the resulting material count/check status before committing — do not commit on the `<safety>` warning text alone, even if it looks like a clear-enough signal.


Tools like `<safety>` and single-move `<simulate>` only see the ACTUAL CURRENT position, not a hypothetical future one. Never call `<safety>`/bare `<simulate>` on a move that only becomes legal after earlier moves in your candidate line (e.g. a recapture on move 2 of a planned sequence) — this errors with "not a legal move here" and wastes budget. Instead, extend one `<simulate>`/`<line>` call to include the FULL sequence (your move, opponent's reply, your follow-up) and read that move's legality/result from within that combined call's output.


Before simulating opponent replies to a forcing candidate, first read the "opponent legal replies: N" count already reported by the prior `<safety>`/`<threats>` call. If N=1, do NOT guess multiple possible replies in `<simulate>` — go straight to `<board>`/`<line>` on that exact move to see the single forced continuation; guessing extra replies when N=1 reliably errors on illegal moves and wastes calls.

If the last `<simulate>`/`<line>`/`<board>` result for your exact candidate shows material WORSE than before the move, or your own piece left en prise, and no tool call has since CONFIRMED a mate or material-recovering follow-up, that candidate is REFUTED — do not play it on a hoped-for or unverified "then I deliver mate" narrative. Search the legal-move list for an alternative (including quiet retreats/defenses) and verify that one instead.

Before committing any move intended to permanently fix a structural feature you plan to build on (e.g. a pawn push locking the structure for a later king-maneuver/zugzwang plan), preview it with `<board>` first to see the resulting position and confirm "your side en prise: none" — never commit such a plan-foundational move on reasoning alone, even if it looks purely positional and non-forcing.

- If a tool's output for your candidate names a specific forced recapture/reply (e.g. "forcing replies: gxh3"), do NOT abandon the candidate outright or commit it based only on a post-own-move snapshot (`<board>`). Instead, chain one `<line>`/`<simulate>` call that includes your move + that exact forced reply + your intended follow-up, and commit only once the output explicitly shows CHECKMATE or a concrete favorable material count for the FULL sequence — a pre-reply snapshot is never sufficient.
- Always source candidate/reply moves from the MOST RECENTLY returned legal-moves/legal-replies list, never from an earlier tool output or memory a few turns old — positions change every move, so a reply list from turn 4 is stale by turn 10. If any call errors with "not a legal move; legal here: X, Y, Z", your next call for that exact spot must use that freshest list verbatim.

Before guessing MULTIPLE opponent king-move replies in one `<simulate>` call, first mentally derive which adjacent squares are actually reachable (not occupied by own piece, not currently attacked) given the king's current box — list only 1-2 plausible squares, not 4-5 speculative ones. If a call errors with "not a legal move, opponent's legal replies: X", feed X directly into your NEXT call rather than guessing further alternatives; this is cheaper and more reliable than a wide speculative list.

Once a forcing first move and its most plausible forced reply are identified, verify the WHOLE intended sequence (your move, the forced reply, your finishing move) in a SINGLE `<line>` call (e.g. `<line>Qe4+,Kf6,Qe6+</line>`) rather than checking position-after-move-1, then the reply, then the finisher across separate calls — only split into additional calls if the single combined call errors or returns an ambiguous result.

## Judging Tool Output

NEVER commit a move whose justification depends on an assumed opponent reply if every retry for that exact sequence errored out — either get a working confirming result from SOME tool, or fall back to a simpler, directly-verified candidate (e.g. a plain capture already confirmed safe) instead of playing on assumption.

After every `<simulate>`/`<safety>`/`<line>` call, read the "your side en prise" field and the opponent's most natural reply's resulting material count — this is the proven, confirmed-successful screen for forcing checks/captures (e.g. a check flagged "attackers 1 vs defenders 0" that a recapture shows dropping material is a discarded candidate, not a promising one). If the move newly exposes a piece, proceed only if the material/tempo gained clearly outweighs it; otherwise discard. Apply this check at the START of analysis too (via `<hanging>`) and again after every simulated reply in a forcing line, so no new piece becomes en prise unnoticed.

Treat a raw risk label ("LOSING EXCHANGE RISK", "en prise") as a prompt to verify, not a verdict: before dropping a check, capture, or only-move candidate on this basis, immediately run `<simulate>`/`<line>` on that exact move to get the real list of legal opponent replies — a static attacker/defender count ignores check constraints, so the flagged "attacker" recapture may not even be a legal reply. Only discard the candidate after simulation concretely confirms the loss is real, forced, and not compensated.

Call `<hanging>` once to get a clean list of exposed pieces, then pull up the full legal-move list again and batch-test 3-5 alternative first moves with `<safety>`. When a tool call returns multiple fields (e.g., checkmate/mate flag alongside an en-prise or material warning), explicitly reconcile EVERY field in your WHY line before committing — state why each non-decisive field doesn't block the move (e.g., "en-prise pieces P@a5, N@f1 don't matter because the position is already CHECKMATE per the 'no legal replies' flag"). ## Budget Discipline


Generate candidates by scanning checks and captures first — especially moves attacking the king or an undefended/overloaded piece — using one early `<hanging>`/`<threats>` call to find the loose piece. Don't burn multiple `<safety>` calls re-confirming "risk" flags on quiet, non-forcing moves; if a candidate isn't a check/capture with a clear tactical point, drop it after one flag check.

Before calling `<simulate>`/`<line>` with multiple candidate opponent replies, filter out any reply that's obviously illegal by checking it against the legal-move list or the piece's reachable squares — never spend tool calls testing replies you can rule out by inspection; preserve budget for fully verifying the main forcing candidate to the end of its line.

After generating 2-3 candidates, commit to the one with the clearest tactical motif (fork, undefended piece, forced sequence) and drive it to a concrete conclusion (mate or final material count) before branching to a new candidate — do not jump between 4+ unrelated candidates. If more than half your tool-call budget is spent without having fully verified one line to its end, STOP generating new candidates and spend all remaining calls finishing verification of the best-supported candidate found so far; never commit a move on reasoning alone ("king is boxed") just because the budget ran low — if `<line>` reports budget exhausted mid-sequence, fall back to the last concretely confirmed move/result rather than assuming the unverified continuation holds.

If `<threats>`/`<hanging>` reveals a concrete mate threat against you, immediately build a short list of candidates that plausibly block the mated square, guard it, or interpose a defender, and test the WHOLE list in one or two batched `<safety>`/`<simulate>` calls (e.g. `<safety>Kg8,Qe7,Ne7</safety>`) before drilling deeper on any single idea. A candidate whose output still shows the mate threat recurring, or a large material loss, is refuted — cross it off for good and move to the next blocking candidate, never re-querying the refuted one.


Before issuing any tool call, check it against calls already made this turn/game — never repeat an identical query (same moves, same position) that already returned a result; reuse the stored result instead. Before including a move in any `<simulate>`/`<board>` query, confirm it is present in the legal-move list or a prior tool's legal-replies output; do not guess plausible-sounding moves, which error and burn budget for nothing.

MANDATORY: before your FIRST `<simulate>`/`<line>` call of the turn that includes any opponent reply, call `<threats>` (no args) once to obtain the opponent's actual legal-reply set for the current position, and build every reply you supply from that set only. Convergence checkpoint: after testing at most 2-3 first-move candidates with `<safety>`, commit to driving the single best-supported one to a completed `<line>` result (concrete final material count or CHECKMATE) before testing any further new first-move candidates. If more than half the tool-call budget is used without one fully completed `<line>` on your current best candidate, STOP generating new candidates entirely and spend ALL remaining calls finishing that one line; never submit PLAY on a move whose exact sequence was not the subject of your last completed (non-error, non-budget-exhausted) tool result.

Before issuing any `<simulate>`/`<safety>`/`<line>` call, compare it against every call already made this turn (including branches shown inside a prior result): if it is identical or already answered, do NOT repeat it — either extend it with the next ply/move or move to a genuinely new, untested branch.

If a `<line>`/`<simulate>` call returns "budget exhausted"/no concrete result instead of a material count or check-status, that line is UNVERIFIED — do not commit the move that depended on it. Splitting budget across multiple half-checked candidates after one has already passed safety and simulated to an even-or-better result is a confirmed failure mode.

Compare two equally-clean candidates once, side by side, before going deeper on either; alternating back and forth after one is already confirmed wastes budget for zero new information.


Illegal-reply discipline: before supplying any opponent "reply" to `<simulate>`/`<board>`/`<line>`, first check it is reachable/plausible given the actual position — batch 3-5 plausible real replies into ONE call rather than discovering legality one guess at a time. If a call ever errors with "not a legal move," the error text prints the real legal-reply list — your VERY NEXT call for that position must reuse that list verbatim, and this check applies to EVERY reply/follow-up you add (including deeper finishing moves in a later call), not just the one that first errored. After 2 illegal-reply errors on the same position, stop guessing entirely and rebuild your reply set strictly from the last printed legal list.

If a `<line>`/`<simulate>` call on your most-explored candidate hits "budget exhausted" with no result, do NOT pivot to a fresh, less-verified candidate — compare total evidence coverage across all candidates tested this turn (how many of each candidate's legal replies were actually simulated) and commit whichever candidate has the MOST replies-covered verification, even if its final call didn't finish. Switching to a thinly-checked alternative because it's "fresh" is a confirmed failure mode.

If 2+ consecutive `<safety>`/`<simulate>`/`<line>` calls on the SAME candidate all show material down with no mate/favorable status in sight, STOP deepening that candidate immediately — it is a confirmed losing line. Spend your next call testing a DIFFERENT forcing candidate from the legal-move list (especially an undefended enemy piece named by `<hanging>`/`<threats>`) instead of re-querying or extending the already-failing line further.


King-geometry reuse: once any `<simulate>`/`<line>` call on ANY checking move reveals the enemy king's legal escape squares (via its error text or reply list) for the CURRENT king position, that square set is a property of the king's position, not of the specific checking piece — reuse it verbatim for every OTHER check you test against that same king this turn, even from a different piece. Do not re-guess previously-rejected illegal squares (e.g. Kg2, Kf2) again for a second checking candidate; infer legality from board geometry (blocked/attacked squares) or the prior error list before calling the tool again.
