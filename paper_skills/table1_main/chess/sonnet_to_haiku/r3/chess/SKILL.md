- If `<safety>`, `<board>`, or any tool output flags a destination/own square as "en prise" or "LOSING EXCHANGE RISK", NEVER commit that move on verbal justification alone, and NEVER discard it on the flag alone either. When you want to verify a candidate finishing move you are not certain is legal or delivers check (e.g., unsure whether "+" or "#" applies), do NOT probe it via `<safety>` with a guessed check/mate suffix — instead run the full hypothesized sequence (your move, forced reply, your next move) in one `<line>` call; the tool reports legality, check status, and mate in that single shot, avoiding wasted illegal-notation retries. This is MANDATORY for every capture, not optional — half a tool budget has been lost to illegal-reply guessing when this step was skipped. Read the literal `opponent legal replies: N` field and trust it exactly — N=1 means the reply is forced; build all subsequent calculation on that single confirmed reply rather than re-deriving or second-guessing it. ## Common Pitfalls



Do not batch candidates into `<safety>`/`<simulate>` straight from the legal-move list, and do not cycle through moves by vague "saw=" observations (pawn structure, undeveloped piece, etc). Before generating candidates, scan explicitly for one of: an undefended attacked piece, a pin/skewer with the enemy king (or queen) behind the attacked piece on a rank/file/diagonal, or a fork square. State that motif in WHY (e.g., "Bc5 pins Qd4 to the king on the g1-a7 diagonal") and restrict testing to moves that create/exploit it, cutting the list to the 1-2 most promising candidates before spending tool calls — never verify many weakly-justified developing/structural moves.

Do NOT abandon a candidate that fits a named motif just because one `<simulate>` reply came back neutral/even. Check every distinct legal retreat/reply square for the targeted piece, not just the first one tested; pick the most forcing or piece-trading reply among them and run `<line>` on it specifically several plies deeper before discarding the candidate. Converge tool budget onto the 1-2 best-motivated candidates early and spend remaining calls deepening those with `<line>`/`<board>`, rather than scattering across many unrelated moves (a repeated, costly failure pattern).


Do NOT discard a forcing check solely because `<safety>`/`<simulate>` flags your own piece "en prise" after it — that flag only counts attackers/defenders, it does not prove a legal black move actually captures. Follow the opponent's forced reply one more ply with `<line>` (e.g. `<line>Rc8+,Kd4,Rxc1></line>`) and check whether any reply in the actual legal-move list captures the flagged piece; discard only if a concrete capture in that line produces a worse-or-equal result than your other candidates.
